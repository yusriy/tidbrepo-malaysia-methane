# Greenhouse gas emissions from ditches in oil palm plantations on tropical peatlands in Malaysia

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

Kasak et al. sampled drainage ditches in first- and second-rotation oil-palm plantations at Betong, Sarawak, on 13–16 April 2023. Floating chambers and laser analysers yielded 86 accepted daytime observations. Concentration spikes distinguished ebullition from diffusion. Drone mapping in October supplied ditch-area estimates.

## Source findings

Table 2 gives pooled **median/mean g CH₄ m⁻² d⁻¹ of ditch water surface**:

| Pathway | Median | Mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| Total | 0.18 | 0.37 |
| Diffusion | 0.06 | 0.12 |
| Ebullition | 0.04 | 0.25 |

Total observations span 0.004–2.83. The abstract instead reports a total median of 0.19; preserve this source discrepancy rather than silently harmonizing it.

## Interpretation and limits

The four-day daytime campaign captures spatial and episodic variability, not a representative annual factor. Flux sampling and area mapping occurred in different months. Distinguish ditch-area methane mass from plantation-area CO₂-equivalent estimates. Scaling requires the ditch fraction; methane and nitrous-oxide GWP assumptions also matter. This supports investigating drainage pathways alongside ecosystem-scale studies, without treating the two measurement footprints as interchangeable.
