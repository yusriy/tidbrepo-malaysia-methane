# Temporal dynamics of soil microbial C and N cycles with GHG fluxes in the transition from tropical peatland forest to oil palm plantation

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

This Applied and Environmental Microbiology article follows forest, land preparation and oil-palm establishment during 2016–2020. Field chambers measured gases using timed samples and gas chromatography. Shotgun metagenomes investigated microbial functions at two soil depths. It appeared online on 23 December 2024 in the January 2025 journal issue.

## Source findings

The “Soil greenhouse gases” results and Fig. 2 show the largest methane fluxes under wet, waterlogged forest conditions, but **no statistically significant methane difference among land-use stages**. The site remained a net methane source through land preparation and plantation establishment. Increased plantation N₂O must not be presented as an equivalent significant CH₄ response.

## Interpretation and limits

Methane-related gene abundance offers mechanistic context alongside measured chamber fluxes; gene counts are not themselves emission rates or proof of causal control. Fig. 2 distinguishes means, medians, Tukey whiskers and outliers. Whiskers are not observed minima/maxima. No exact flux values were digitized from figures and the supplementary numeric table was not independently reviewed. Use this note for process interpretation; retrieve original numeric evidence before adding a quantitative claim. Check site/time overlap before combining it with other Sarawak conversion studies.
