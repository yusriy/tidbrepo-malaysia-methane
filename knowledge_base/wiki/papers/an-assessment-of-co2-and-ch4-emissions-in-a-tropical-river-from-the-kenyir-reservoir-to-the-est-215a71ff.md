# An assessment of CO2 and CH4 emissions in a tropical river: from the Kenyir Reservoir to the estuary

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

The study follows the Kenyir Reservoir–Terengganu River–estuary continuum in 2017–2019. Dissolved gases were measured by gas chromatography. Diffusive fluxes were **calculated** from concentration gradients and published gas-transfer parameterizations; local k₆₀₀ was not measured. Separate submerged funnels sampled reservoir ebullition over 24 hours.

## Source findings

Results report reservoir diffusive CH₄ ranges of **0.02–5.0 dry-season** and **0.1–12 wet-season mmol m⁻² d⁻¹**. Table 1 instead gives a pooled upper value of 12.5; preserve the different source presentations. Reported reservoir ebullition is **3.79 × 10⁻² dry** and **3.94 × 10⁻⁸ wet**, in the same units. The very small wet-season value describes bubbling, not generic river diffusion.

## Interpretation and limits

Separate diffusion, ebullition, locations, seasons and table statistic labels before comparison; Table 2 includes median-based summaries. Gas-transfer assumptions and sparse bubble sampling limit scaling. Urban runoff, sand mining and dam-related spatial patterns are observational associations. The work informs aquatic methane pathways, not a single universal annual waterbody factor. Preserve original units alongside approved standardized database units; no factor or conversion was changed.
