# Long-term effects of nitrogen fertilization on methane emissions in drained tropical peatland

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

Chaddy et al. studied drained oil-palm peatland near Sibu, Sarawak. Three randomized blocks received 0, 31.1, 62.2 or 124.3 kg N ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ as ammonium sulfate. Monthly closed chambers and gas chromatography covered 2010–2013 and 2016–2017; 2014–2015 were not measured. Online publication was 18 December 2025; the final journal version is dated 20 January 2026.

## Source findings

The nitrogen-rate effect on methane was not significant (p = 0.680); the year effect was significant (p = 0.001). Table 2 uses **µg C m⁻² h⁻¹**, with **SE**, not CH₄ mass or SD.

Its printed mean row reads T1 59.1 ± 9.7, T2 64.4 ± 12.2, T3 3.96 ± 8.3 and T4 4.21 ± 5.6. The last two central values appear inconsistent with the yearly entries. They remain literal source values; do not silently move decimal points.

## Interpretation and limits

This supports a site- and fertilizer-specific null treatment result, not a universal claim that nitrogen cannot affect methane. Redox and root-exudate explanations remain inferred. Consult the yearly data or authors before using the questionable aggregate cells.
