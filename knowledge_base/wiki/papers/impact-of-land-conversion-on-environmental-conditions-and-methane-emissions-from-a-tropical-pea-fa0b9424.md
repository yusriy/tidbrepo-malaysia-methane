# Impact of land conversion on environmental conditions and methane emissions from a tropical peatland

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

Wong et al. followed a Sarawak peat-swamp forest through conversion, January 2014–December 2020. Eddy covariance measured ecosystem CH₄ exchange, with single-height storage correction. Only 34% of observations passed quality control; marginal distribution sampling filled gaps. Burning events were excluded from gap-filling and routine comparisons.

## Source findings

Table 2 reports monthly means ± SD in **mg C m⁻² d⁻¹**, not CH₄ mass:

| Phase | Dates | Exchange |
|---|---|---|
| Before | Jan 2014–Feb 2017 | 12.1 ± 5.3 |
| During | Mar 2017–Apr 2018 | 23.3 ± 8.6 |
| After | May 2018–Dec 2020 | 16.3 ± 4.1 |

## Interpretation and limits

Drainage accompanied higher ecosystem emissions. Ditches are a proposed explanation, not a directly measured source partition. Burning peaks use seconds as the time denominator and cannot substitute for daily background factors. Extensive gap-filling, changing tower height and one-site coverage limit extrapolation. Check overlapping site/time coverage with earlier Wong studies before pooling observations. No standardized database units were changed.
