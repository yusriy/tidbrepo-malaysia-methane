# Capturing Biogas: A Means to Reduce Green House Gas Emissions for the Production of Crude Palm Oil

> Full-text synthesis checked against the source on 2 November 2026. This status does not approve new factors or change existing database records.

## Study and methods

Vijaya, Ma and Choo analysed gate-to-gate crude palm-oil production at 12 Malaysian mills. Methane estimates use an assumed biogas yield of **28 m³ per tonne POME**, citing earlier work. They are inventory calculations, not new direct methane measurements at every mill.

## Source findings

Table 3 gives **22.21 kg methane per tonne CPO allocated**, versus **36.41 unallocated**. Allocation changes the product burden, not the underlying physical emissions. Tables 4–5 compare no-capture and 85%-capture scenarios: allocated total GHG burdens are 602.18 and 137.36 kg CO₂eq per tonne CPO. These totals are not methane-mass factors. Table 1 uses a historical methane GWP of 23.

## Interpretation and limits

The abstract/methods describe a three-year inventory, while results refer to five years; retain that inconsistency. Mill coverage, assumed biogas generation, allocation and capture efficiency constrain transfer to other operations. Use the study for mitigation and inventory-boundary context. Do not substitute its modeled capture scenario for a measured present-day national factor, or compare CO₂-equivalent burdens directly with standardized methane mass.
